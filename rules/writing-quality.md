# Writing Quality

Apply these rules to all prose output: responses, reports, review findings, comments,
commit messages, and drafted communications. Two standards work together: ASD-STE100
governs sentence mechanics, Zinsser governs overall quality.

## ASD-STE100 (Simplified Technical English)

Apply the STE writing rules. The STE controlled dictionary is not enforced: security and
domain terms count as technical names and are allowed. Keep the general vocabulary simple.

- Use the active voice. Name the actor.
- Keep sentences short. One clause carrying one fact reads faster than a compound sentence.
- Write one topic per sentence. Write one instruction per sentence in procedures.
- Keep each paragraph to one topic and a maximum of 6 sentences.
- Use the same term for the same thing everywhere. Give one word one meaning.
- Do not omit necessary words (articles, verbs) to make text shorter.
- Use a vertical list for a sequence of three or more steps or items.
- Put warnings and conditions before the action they apply to.
- Use the imperative for instructions ("Run the script", not "The script should be run").

## Zinsser's four principles (On Writing Well)

- **Simplicity**: strip every sentence to its cleanest components. Cut clutter: qualifiers,
  filler phrases, and jargon that a plain word can replace.
- **Brevity**: prefer the short word and the short sentence. Say it once. If a sentence
  adds no decision-relevant fact, delete it.
- **Clarity**: the reader must never read a sentence twice. Order ideas logically. Be
  precise: name the thing instead of pointing at it with "this" or "it".
- **Humanity**: write like a person, for a person. Answer the reader's actual question.
  Do not let structure or formality push out warmth.

## Precedence

When rules conflict, clarity for the audience wins. STE mechanics serve Zinsser's
principles; do not let rigid mechanics make text colder or harder to read.
